Budget-Aware Step Selection for Forward-Only Recovery of Analog Accelerators

Anonymous authors

  • Paper · available after review
  • Code · released with the paper
  • BibTeX

Recovering a drifted analog accelerator with forward passes alone spends device queries twice: on the updates, and on finding a step size that works. We model how readout noise accumulates over a finite budget, use that model to choose the step from other devices' measurements, and count every query against searching each device or reusing a fixed setting.

  • Descent ≠ survival 27.2×

    Measured single-step descent boundaries exceed whole-run failure boundaries by this geometric mean over 27 testbed comparisons.

  • Noise-displacement model 2.20×

    Geometric-mean overprediction of failure boundaries over 21 settings; the measured direction-count and noise exponents are +0.969 and −1.059.

  • Controlled study, 36 targets −93.6% forwards

    Fine/cohort donor selection reaches 95.07% against 95.47% for target search, a 0.399 pp deficit, using 1.22M instead of 19.08M operational forwards.

The saving comes from avoiding target search, not from the donor measurements themselves. A transferred cohort-specific fixed setting reaches 95.39% at lower incremental cost (results).

At a glance

Choosing the step from other devices

ScopeThe bound covers an idealized noise-only process, not device failure. Radius calibration uses clean simulator readout, which physical devices may not provide.

Recovery can write the device's parameters and read a noisy scalar loss, but it cannot compute gradients, so it uses two-point zeroth-order updates with probe ε\Seps and step η\Seta. The obvious step, the largest one that still gives expected descent, fails: measured single-step descent boundaries sit 27.2× (geometric mean) above the largest step that survives a whole run.

Readout noise persists even when the useful update becomes small, so it accumulates over the run. Bounding how far a noise-only process drifts in T=B/2mT=\SB/2\Sm updates (Proposition 1) gives a step scale:

ηnoise(m,B)=2 R ε mσrD B\eta_{\mathrm{noise}}(\Sm,\SB)=\frac{2\,\SR\,\Seps\,\Sm}{\Ssig\sqrt{\SD\,\SB}}

Other devices of the same substrate, called donors, supply the readout noise σr\Ssig and the radius R\SR. Their pooled coefficient κ\kappa sets a ceiling q η0≤δ εmκq\,\eta_0\le\sqrt\delta\,\Seps\Sm\kappa, where qq accounts for the step schedule, and the rule takes the largest rate on a ladder below it, or abstains. Does the model predict when a run fails?

In a controlled testbed we measured the largest step that survives a full run for 21 settings of noise level and direction count, then compared it with each model's prediction. Coefficients are measured, never fitted to failure. The noise model overpredicts by 2.20× in geometric mean, while the two predictions from single-step descent give ratios of 0.00377× and 11.7× (right panel). The measured exponents (+0.969 for m\Sm, −1.059 for σr\Ssig) match the model's form, but its predicted budget dependence B−1/2\SB^{-1/2} is not confirmed.

Accuracy and the queries it costs

On 36 held-out crossbar targets with disjoint donors, every policy is frozen before recovery. Every forward pass is counted: calibration, search, recovery, monitoring and validation.

How to read itA descriptive accuracy–cost trade-off, not evidence of non-inferiority: the 0.399 pp deficit exceeds the prespecified 0.10 pp tolerance.

Fine/cohort selection reaches 95.07% against 95.47% for target search, with 93.62% fewer operational forwards. Whether the donor measurements themselves help depends on the comparison: fine/cohort beats the fixed default by 0.554 pp, but a transferred cohort-specific fixed setting reaches 95.39% at lower cost, and the coarse rule picks exactly the fixed default on all 36 targets.

Limitations

  • Simulation onlyAll hardware results are simulations on small classification tasks. Physical devices and larger models are untested.
  • Clean calibrationRadius calibration and development scoring use clean simulator readout.
  • An idealized boundIt covers noise-only displacement, not device reliability, and the budget exponent is unresolved.
  • A small studyThree base models; query counts are not hardware energy savings.
